FCC Ads — Business Monitoring
Oct 2026  ·  Scope: Business and reporting RCA for FCC and Client (Nahdi) — technical RCA is out of scope of this document.
The One Number
Ad Revenue
The top-line outcome of the ads business. Ad Revenue is a function of Supply and Demand — bounded by whichever side is the limiting constraint. High supply with low demand leaves inventory unfilled. High demand with low supply means advertisers can't reach audiences. Growth requires both sides to scale together.
Total ad revenue — Nahdi platform Total ad revenue — FCC view WoW / MoM trend Revenue per 1,000 ad requests (RPM)
Ad Revenue = f(Supply, Demand)
A drop in Ad Revenue means either supply has contracted, demand has weakened, or both. The sections below diagnose which.
If Supply is the constraint
Traffic exists on the platform but there isn't enough reach for advertisers to buy against. Ad requests are low, impressions available are low. → See Supply section below.
If Demand is the constraint
Traffic exists but advertisers aren't buying or aren't able to. Could be motivation (low ROAS, low CTR) or ability (low campaigns, idle budgets). → See Demand section below.
Supply Is there enough traffic for ads to run against?
Supply is the attention base — the searches and page views that create ad opportunities. The drill path starts at platform traffic, narrows to what FCC sees (ad requests by product), then to how much of that is being filled.
Platform traffic — out of scope for now: Upstream supply metrics (total search volume, page views, sessions, active users on Nahdi) are not currently trackable by FCC — these are owned by Nahdi. Ideally these would be visible here to give full supply context. Flagged as a data access dependency to resolve.
Ad Requests — what FCC sees
Metric What it tells you If it drops, drill into
Total ad requests
PLA + Display combined
 Volume of ad opportunities reaching FCC. Best proxy for platform supply when upstream traffic data isn't available. Split by product type below
Ad requests — PLA
Product Listing Ads (CPC)
 Search-driven ad requests. A drop here means fewer searches are triggering PLA calls — either search volume dropped or the integration broke. Platform search volume (Nahdi-side); serving error rate (Serving Eval tab)
Ad requests — Display
Display Ads (CPM)
 Page-view-driven ad requests. A drop here means fewer page views are generating display impression opportunities. Platform page view volume (Nahdi-side); display placement config
Fill Rate
Ads served / Total ad requests
 Of the requests FCC receives, how many result in a served ad. The bridge between supply and demand — low fill rate means either no matching campaign (demand gap) or pipeline failure (technical). Demand gap → Demand section. Pipeline failure → Serving Eval tab
Demand Are advertisers buying, and are they able to?
Demand is driven by advertiser behaviour — whether they're motivated to spend and whether they're set up to do so. A drop in demand is either a motivation problem (ads aren't delivering value) or an ability problem (advertisers can't configure or scale campaigns).
Motivation Are advertisers getting value from their spend?
If motivation is low, advertisers reduce budgets or stop campaigning. The drill path follows the value chain — from top-line return down to the individual components that drive it.
Motivation Metrics — drill top-down
Metric What it tells you If it drops, drill into
ROAS
Revenue attributed to ad clicks / Ad spend
 Top-line return for advertisers. The primary motivation signal — strong ROAS drives budget increases; weak ROAS causes pullback. CVR dropping or Clicks dropping?
Incremental Sales
Sales caused by the ad, above organic baseline
 True ad-driven revenue lift — proves causality, not just correlation. Stronger motivation signal than ROAS alone. Requires holdout measurement — methodology TBD
CVR (Conversion Rate)
Orders / Clicks
 Are clicks leading to purchases? A drop here with stable CTR means the product or landing experience isn't converting — advertiser-side issue. Product-query relevance, landing page, pricing
CTR (Click-Through Rate)
Clicks / Impressions served
 Are users engaging with the ads shown? A drop here means the served ad isn't relevant or compelling enough to act on. Impressions stable? → relevance problem (Serving Eval tab). Impressions dropped? → fill rate / supply
Total Clicks
PLA + Display
 Raw click volume. A drop can come from fewer impressions (supply) or lower CTR (relevance). Decompose before concluding. Impressions dropped → supply. CTR dropped → Serving Eval tab
Total Impressions Served
PLA + Display
 Volume of ads actually shown to users. Function of fill rate × ad requests. A drop is either supply falling or fill rate falling. Ad requests dropped → supply. Fill rate dropped → demand gap or pipeline
Share of Voice
Advertiser's impressions / Total impressions available
 How much of available ad inventory a given advertiser is capturing. Per-advertiser metric. A drop signals increasing competition or budget exhaustion. Budget exhausted early → Ability section. More competitors → competitive context
Ability Are advertisers set up to spend effectively?
Even motivated advertisers can't spend if they're not set up correctly — too few campaigns, exhausted budgets, sparse keyword coverage, or a product that's hard to manage. A healthy motivation score with weak ability metrics means the platform is leaving money on the table.
Ability Metrics
Metric What it tells you If it drops / is low
Active advertisers
Brands with at least one live campaign
 Breadth of demand. If low or shrinking, the revenue base is fragile — too concentrated in too few advertisers. Onboarding gap or churn — check new advertiser additions vs. deactivations
Active campaigns
Campaigns in live / running state
 Depth of demand. An advertiser with one campaign is fragile. More campaigns across more categories = more robust demand. Paused or ended campaigns — check budget exhaustion rate
Active keywords
Keywords triggered at least once in last 7 days
 How much of configured keyword inventory is actually live. High idle rate reduces coverage and fill rate. Keyword-query mismatch or low search volume for configured terms
Total keywords configured
Across all live campaigns
 Coverage of demand. Low keyword count relative to search volume = large share of queries with no campaign match. Compare against top search queries — identify uncovered head terms
Total listings in campaigns
Unique products across all live campaigns
 Product coverage in ads. Low listing count = narrow advertiser participation or sparse catalogue coverage. Check % of Nahdi catalogue covered by at least one campaign
Budget utilisation rate
Spend / Configured budget
 Are advertisers spending their full allocated budget? Low utilisation = bids too low or keywords not matching. High utilisation early = budget cap limiting spend. Low → bid or keyword gap. High with early exhaustion → budget cap too tight
Time to set up a campaign
Median time from creation to first impression
 Ease of onboarding. Long setup time signals product friction suppressing demand before it starts. Review onboarding flow for drop-off points
Time to edit / manage a campaign
Median time for common management actions
 Ease of ongoing use. High management time discourages active optimisation, leading to idle budgets and stale keywords. Review campaign management flows for friction
Reading motivation and ability together: Motivation dropping with ability stable → product isn't delivering returns, focus on relevance and conversion. Ability dropping with motivation stable → advertisers want to spend but can't, focus on onboarding and campaign tooling. Both dropping → structural demand problem, needs coordinated fix.
FCC Ads — Serving Eval Framework
Oct 2026  ·  Scope: Ad serving only — campaign setup, attribution, and reporting are out of scope of this document.
North Star
What Are We Measuring?
The objective of an ad is to increase monetisation within the limits of relevance. The mechanism is visibility — getting the right ad in front of the right user at the right moment. ROAS is the business outcome that captures whether this is working end-to-end.
ROAS = Revenue attributed to ad clicks / Ad spend
Equivalently: ROAS = CVR × ASP / CPC  |  CVR = conversion rate  |  ASP = avg selling price  |  CPC = cost per click
FCC's operational metric — Fill Rate: Of the levers in ROAS (CVR, ASP, CPC), FCC Serving controls none of them directly. What FCC controls is whether an ad is served at all. Fill Rate = ads served / total ad requests. It is the upstream input that determines how many clicks are even possible. This framework tracks ROAS as the business north star and Fill Rate as the primary FCC-owned operational metric beneath it.
Incrementality caveat: ROAS here is attributed ROAS — revenue from orders placed after an ad click. Whether those buyers would have converted without the ad requires organic conversion data and holdout experiments. Known limitation, deferred.
Decomposition
When ROAS Shows a Gap — Where to Look
Clicks — the bridge between serving and revenue — are a function of four factors. FCC Serving directly owns one of them.
# Clicks = Traffic × Ad Slots × Fill Rate × CTR
Traffic & Ad Slots = retailer-controlled (Nahdi)  |  Fill Rate = FCC Serving  |  CTR = shared (FCC relevance + advertiser creative)
Traffic & Slots
Set by the retailer. Monitored as context — not a serving fix target.
Fill Rate drops
FCC is not serving ads for queries it should be serving. Primary FCC-owned metric. Diagnosed in Level 3 below.
CTR drops
Ads served but users not clicking. Partially FCC-owned (which product surfaces) and partially advertiser-owned (creative). Diagnosed in Level 2 below.
Conversion Rate — deferred: Even with Fill Rate and CTR healthy, ROAS can drop if clicks don't convert. CVR is post-click and advertiser-controlled. This framework does not diagnose CVR. If ROAS drops while Fill Rate and CTR are stable, conversion is the likely cause — tracked as a signal but not drilled into here.
Level 2 — Relevance
Was What We Served Relevant?
When CTR is low, ads are being served but users aren't clicking. The goal is to identify which segment of queries is driving the low CTR — not to audit every served ad.
1
Overall CTR — baseline
Track weekly. Any sustained drop triggers investigation.
Next: Segment by query volume tier.
↓
2
CTR by query volume tier
Segment queries into head (top 20% by volume), torso, and tail. Head queries drive ~80% of traffic — a drop there is systemic. A drop only in tail is more contained.
Next: Break down the flagged tier(s) by match type.
↓
3
CTR by match type
Exact match should have higher CTR than phrase match. If exact match CTR is low, the served product isn't matching the keyword well. If phrase match CTR is low, matching is too broad.
Next: Break down the flagged match type(s) by ad position.
↓
4
CTR by position
Position 1 should get significantly more clicks than positions 2, 3, etc. If CTR across positions is flat, ranking isn't putting the right product first.
Next: Relevance Reviewer — weekly sample of (query, served product) pairs from the segment(s) flagged above.
Level 3 — Fill Rate
Should We Have Served Something But Didn't?
When Fill Rate is low, the question is: for the queries where nothing was served, was there a relevant campaign that should have matched? This decomposition separates pipeline failures (FCC's problem) from demand gaps (advertiser's problem).
1
Take all no-ad queries from the query log
Every search that returned no served ad is the starting universe. This is the full Fill Rate miss set.
Next: Run offline keyword match against active campaigns.
↓
2
Run keyword matching logic offline against active campaigns
For each no-ad query, check whether an active campaign has a keyword that should match (exact or phrase). Use a snapshot of campaigns at query time.
Next: Bucket each query into one of two outcomes.
↓
3
Bucket the results
Keyword should have matched, no ad served — confirmed pipeline failure. Demand existed; FCC did not serve. Volume here is the false negative rate — the primary serving health signal.

No keyword matched — either a demand gap (advertiser hasn't configured this keyword) or a genuine no-match (no relevant product in catalogue). Distinguishing these requires a catalogue-level relevance check — deferred.
Next: Relevance Reviewer on confirmed false negatives to validate whether a relevant ad should have been served.
↓
4
Track false negative rate weekly
False negative rate = confirmed pipeline failures / total no-ad queries. An upward trend means the serving pipeline is dropping queries it should be catching.
Next: Continue sampling into the Relevance Reviewer while this rate is elevated.
Relevance Reviewer
External Relevance Check
Offline check using the search query and catalogue data (product name, description, category), independent of the system's internal relevance score. Supports two checks: whether a relevant ad should have been served for a no-ad query (Level 3), and whether a served product was relevant (Level 2). Same four approaches; the input pairs differ by case.
1
Lexical match — baseline filter
Check whether query terms appear in the product name, description, or category. Fast floor check before deeper review.
Next: Embedding similarity on pairs that pass or need scoring.
2
Embedding similarity — primary signal
Multilingual vectors for query and product text (Arabic + English). Score similarity; flag pairs below threshold for review.
Next: LLM review on flagged or borderline pairs.
3
LLM-as-judge — ambiguous cases
Structured relevance rating (1–3) with a short reason for pairs that embedding similarity did not resolve.
Next: Track trend on the golden set.
4
Golden set + NDCG — trend metric
~100 high-traffic queries with human-labelled correct products. Run weekly; NDCG@5 shows whether retrieval and ranking improve over time.
Next: Publish weekly: should-serve miss rate (no-ad / false-negative samples), irrelevance rate (served samples), and NDCG@5.
