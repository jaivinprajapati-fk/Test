FCC Ads — Serving Eval Framework
Oct 2026  ·  Scope: Ad serving only — campaign setup, attribution, and reporting are out of scope of this document.
North Star
What Are We Measuring?
The objective of an ad is to increase monetisation within the limits of relevance. The mechanism is visibility — getting the right ad in front of the right user at the right moment. ROAS is the business outcome that captures whether this is working end-to-end.
ROAS = Revenue attributed to ad clicks / Ad spend
Equivalently: ROAS = CVR × ASP / CPC  |  CVR = conversion rate  |  ASP = avg selling price  |  CPC = cost per click
FCC's operational metric — Fill Rate: Of the levers in ROAS (CVR, ASP, CPC), FCC Serving controls none of them directly. What FCC controls is whether an ad is served at all. Fill Rate = ads served / total ad requests. It is the upstream input that determines how many clicks are even possible. This framework tracks ROAS as the business north star and Fill Rate as the primary FCC-owned operational metric beneath it.
Incrementality caveat: ROAS here is attributed ROAS — revenue from orders placed after an ad click. Whether those buyers would have converted without the ad requires organic conversion data and holdout experiments. Known limitation, deferred.
Decomposition
When ROAS Shows a Gap — Where to Look
Clicks — the bridge between serving and revenue — are a function of four factors. FCC Serving directly owns one of them.
# Clicks = Traffic × Ad Slots × Fill Rate × CTR
Traffic & Ad Slots = retailer-controlled (Nahdi)  |  Fill Rate = FCC Serving  |  CTR = shared (FCC relevance + advertiser creative)
Traffic & Slots
Set by the retailer. Monitored as context — not a serving fix target.
Fill Rate drops
FCC is not serving ads for queries it should be serving. Primary FCC-owned metric. Diagnosed in Level 3 below.
CTR drops
Ads served but users not clicking. Partially FCC-owned (which product surfaces) and partially advertiser-owned (creative). Diagnosed in Level 2 below.
Conversion Rate — deferred: Even with Fill Rate and CTR healthy, ROAS can drop if clicks don't convert. CVR is post-click and advertiser-controlled. This framework does not diagnose CVR. If ROAS drops while Fill Rate and CTR are stable, conversion is the likely cause — tracked as a signal but not drilled into here.
Level 2 — Relevance
Was What We Served Relevant?
When CTR is low, ads are being served but users aren't clicking. The goal is to identify which segment of queries is driving the low CTR — not to audit every served ad.
1
Overall CTR — baseline
Track weekly. Any sustained drop triggers investigation.
Next: Segment by query volume tier.
↓
2
CTR by query volume tier
Segment queries into head (top 20% by volume), torso, and tail. Head queries drive ~80% of traffic — a drop there is systemic. A drop only in tail is more contained.
Next: Break down the flagged tier(s) by match type.
↓
3
CTR by match type
Exact match should have higher CTR than phrase match. If exact match CTR is low, the served product isn't matching the keyword well. If phrase match CTR is low, matching is too broad.
Next: Break down the flagged match type(s) by ad position.
↓
4
CTR by position
Position 1 should get significantly more clicks than positions 2, 3, etc. If CTR across positions is flat, ranking isn't putting the right product first.
Next: Relevance Reviewer — weekly sample of (query, served product) pairs from the segment(s) flagged above.
Level 3 — Fill Rate
Should We Have Served Something But Didn't?
When Fill Rate is low, the question is: for the queries where nothing was served, was there a relevant campaign that should have matched? This decomposition separates pipeline failures (FCC's problem) from demand gaps (advertiser's problem).
1
Take all no-ad queries from the query log
Every search that returned no served ad is the starting universe. This is the full Fill Rate miss set.
Next: Run offline keyword match against active campaigns.
↓
2
Run keyword matching logic offline against active campaigns
For each no-ad query, check whether an active campaign has a keyword that should match (exact or phrase). Use a snapshot of campaigns at query time.
Next: Bucket each query into one of two outcomes.
↓
3
Bucket the results
Keyword should have matched, no ad served — confirmed pipeline failure. Demand existed; FCC did not serve. Volume here is the false negative rate — the primary serving health signal.

No keyword matched — either a demand gap (advertiser hasn't configured this keyword) or a genuine no-match (no relevant product in catalogue). Distinguishing these requires a catalogue-level relevance check — deferred.
Next: Relevance Reviewer on confirmed false negatives to validate whether a relevant ad should have been served.
↓
4
Track false negative rate weekly
False negative rate = confirmed pipeline failures / total no-ad queries. An upward trend means the serving pipeline is dropping queries it should be catching.
Next: Continue sampling into the Relevance Reviewer while this rate is elevated.
Relevance Reviewer
External Relevance Check
Offline check using the search query and catalogue data (product name, description, category), independent of the system's internal relevance score. Supports two checks: whether a relevant ad should have been served for a no-ad query (Level 3), and whether a served product was relevant (Level 2). Same four approaches; the input pairs differ by case.
1
Lexical match — baseline filter
Check whether query terms appear in the product name, description, or category. Fast floor check before deeper review.
Next: Embedding similarity on pairs that pass or need scoring.
2
Embedding similarity — primary signal
Multilingual vectors for query and product text (Arabic + English). Score similarity; flag pairs below threshold for review.
Next: LLM review on flagged or borderline pairs.
3
LLM-as-judge — ambiguous cases
Structured relevance rating (1–3) with a short reason for pairs that embedding similarity did not resolve.
Next: Track trend on the golden set.
4
Golden set + NDCG — trend metric
~100 high-traffic queries with human-labelled correct products. Run weekly; NDCG@5 shows whether retrieval and ranking improve over time.
Next: Publish weekly: should-serve miss rate (no-ad / false-negative samples), irrelevance rate (served samples), and NDCG@5.
Summary
Metrics to Track, In Order
Track these weekly. Each metric only requires investigation when it moves — you don't audit everything all the time.
Metric Owner What It Tells You Triggers Investigation When
ROAS Business Overall advertiser value — revenue per unit of spend Drops week-over-week; decompose into Fill Rate, CTR, Conv. Rate
Fill Rate FCC Serving Share of ad requests that result in a served ad Drops — then Level 3 false negative analysis
CTR FCC + Advertiser Whether served ads are relevant enough for users to click Drops — then segment by tier, match type, position (Level 2)
Conversion Rate Advertiser Whether clicks result in orders. Post-click — outside Levels 2–3. If ROAS drops while Fill Rate and CTR are stable, this is the likely cause. Drops while Fill Rate and CTR are stable
False Negative Rate FCC Serving Share of no-ad queries where a campaign existed and should have matched Rises — pipeline is dropping queries it should be serving
