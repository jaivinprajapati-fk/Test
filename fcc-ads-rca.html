Sanity Check — Banner Group Coverage
Are the 140 Active BGs Actually Serving?
How coverage is measured: For each BG, every configured keyword was searched individually. Coverage % = what fraction of the BG's products appeared in results across all those searches. 100% means every product showed up for at least one keyword. 0% means no product appeared for any keyword. Important: even 100% here doesn't mean everything is fine — it only means products are reachable. Whether the right keyword is serving the right product is a separate question we address next.

Next step: Engineering to identify why these 17 BGs return nothing — check catalogue sync, Solr indexing, and keyword-product alignment for each. Fix root cause per BG.

Next step: Investigate after the 17 zero-coverage BGs are resolved.

Where Do Keywords Fail in the Serving Pipeline?
For each of the 1,571 keywords in the report, we trace how far it gets through the serving pipeline. Each step is a potential drop-off point. Here's the breakdown.

Next step: Engineering to confirm why these 6 keywords are missing from the KWT index and re-index them.
Step 2b — Category Classification: Does the query land in the right category?
For the 1,565 indexed keywords, the system runs category classification on the query. Only keywords whose detected category matches the banner group's category proceed. There is no fallback — a mismatch is a hard fail.
486
Category matched — proceed
549

530

Issue — largest drop-off in the pipeline: 1,079 of 1,565 keywords (69%) fail at category classification. Of these, 549 get classified into a wrong category and 530 return no category at all. Both are hard fails — no ad is served. This single step is blocking the majority of keywords from ever reaching retrieval.
Next step: Engineering to audit the classifier separately for wrong-category (549) and no-detection (530) cases. Confirm whether spell correction is a contributing factor before fixing — these two workstreams may have the same root cause.
Step 2c — Spell Correction: Is the query being altered?
Before category classification runs, spell correction processes the raw keyword. This step changes the query text — which can affect both category detection and product retrieval.
1,010
Keywords altered by spell correction (64%)
555
Keywords unchanged
Issue: 64% of keywords are being modified by spell correction. Several alterations look incorrect — "HAIRFALL" → "hairfal", "MINERAL" → "miner", "AVEENO BODY" → "aveeno bodi". Of the 1,010 altered keywords, only 325 (32%) still pass category matching. The correlation with category failure is significant.
Next step: Engineering to validate spell correction accuracy on this keyword set and confirm whether it should be disabled or tuned for brand/product keywords. This should be done in parallel with the category classifier audit (Step 2b).
Step 2d — Product Retrieval: Does Solr return anything?
For the 486 keywords that pass category matching, product Solr is queried. This step should return a ranked list of relevant products.

Issue: 43 keywords pass category classification but Solr returns zero products. Examples: "ACRETIN CREAM", "AVEENO BODY", "BIODERMA TONER", "ECZEMA CREAM". Some have brand signals detected, yet retrieval still fails. Points to a product catalogue gap or Solr index issue.
Next step: Engineering to check whether these 43 keywords' products exist in the catalogue and are indexed in Solr. If missing from catalogue — client to add. If indexed but not returned — Solr retrieval logic issue.
Step 2e — Intersection: Do retrieved products belong to the configured BG?
Product Solr and BG Solr results are intersected. Only products that appear in both are eligible to be served. This is the final gate before ranking.
299

124

How to read these numbers: Each number is a keyword count, not a product count. For 299 keywords — when searched, every product in that BG appeared in Solr results at least once. For 124 keywords — some BG products came back but others didn't. For example, keyword "EUCERIN DERMOPURIFYER" has 17 products in its BG; Solr only returned 1 of them (5.8% coverage). The other 16 products won't be served when a user searches that term. For 20 keywords — Solr returned products, but none belonged to the configured BG at all.
Issue: 20 keywords retrieve products but none belong to the configured BG — the intersection is empty. In some cases extra (unconfigured) BG IDs appear in BG Solr, suggesting their products may have been served instead. In others there are no extra BGs — the intersection simply fails, pointing to a product-BG mapping or indexing gap.
Next step: Engineering to check product-BG mapping integrity for these 20 keywords and confirm whether any unconfigured BG products were actually served to users.
Step 2f — BG Solr Over-retrieval: Are unconfigured BGs leaking in?
Separately from the intersection outcome, BG Solr is returning banner group IDs that were not configured for that keyword in 516 cases. This is a potential over-serving signal — if those BGs' products also appear in product Solr, they could be served to users.

Next step: Engineering to cross-check BG Solr over-retrieval against serving logs and confirm how many of the 516 resulted in actual impressions from unconfigured BGs.
